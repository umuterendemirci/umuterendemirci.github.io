Unstitched

Unravel crocheted toys, stitch by stitch.

A hyper-casual 3D puzzle for phones, inspired by Yarn Fever! Unravel Puzzle. Tap a part with a loose end and its next round comes off stitch by stitch onto an open spool of its colour; with no spool open it waits in a tray of 0 to 3 places, and a full tray tangles the toy. Sewn-on parts must go first, and from level 5 a dress, a sweater or a bigger doll hides what is underneath, with an eye that shows one layer down.

I designed the rules, the sewing and the layers, the 13 toys and their order, and the look. Every toy is built from its stitches in code, with no 3D model. A tuner visits every position a toy can reach, and I chose each toy's spool order and tray from what it measured.

How it's designed

Each part below condenses one chapter of the game design document. Every summary ends with a link to the full chapter in the PDF.

01The game at a glance

Look, pull, fill, unravel. Turn the toy to see which parts are free, read the queue of spools, and pull: four rounds fill a spool, it pops and the next opens. Take every round off to win; if nothing can be pulled, the toy tangles and Again is free.

  • It comes apart like crochet: stitch by stitch, round by round.
  • Every rule shows on the toy: loose ends, sewing, layers.
  • Hidden, but fair: the eye shows one layer down.
Read the full chapter in the GDD (page 1)

02Parts, spools, the tray and layers

A toy is parts made of rounds. A part sewn on another must go before the rounds beneath it, and tapping a stuck part names what holds it. Two spools are open at a time; the tray holds the rest. Layers hide colours from level 5, and the eye turns them see-through one layer at a time.

Read the full chapter in the GDD (page 2)

03Thirteen toys, one new idea each

From a chick that can't be lost to a doll inside a doll inside a doll. A random player wins every game on the first two toys and about one in ten on the last; a careful player who pulls whatever fits does even worse on the snowman, bear, rocket and doll, where reading ahead matters.

Read the full chapter in the GDD (page 3)

04How difficulty is measured and chosen

Toys are data. ToyReport visits every position a toy can reach and walks back from the wins, so it knows which are lost. The tuner measures spool orders and trays; most orders turn out too safe (84% of the bear's sampled orders can't be lost), so I choose each queue on purpose.

Read the full chapter in the GDD (page 4)

05What the measurements and the builds showed

The first test toy held no decision; tags gave way to tapping the toy itself; layers were tried on the bear alone; the gift and doll were re-sewn. Measured again with 1,000 games, the rocket is easier than the bear before it. Unstitched has not been playtested by first-time players yet.

Read the full chapter in the GDD (page 5)

06Feel, art and next steps

Stitches lift before they let go, a 3D rope reels into a spool that squashes and pops, and the toy rocks on each pull. A hand-written wool shader on fibres from Poly Haven; MintoDog's music, Kenney's buttons. A monetisation plan (not built), the first KPIs and a roadmap close the document.
